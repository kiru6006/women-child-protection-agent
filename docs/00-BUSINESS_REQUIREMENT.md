# Proposal for Government of India

## AI-Powered Women & Child Protection, Policy & Service Gap Intelligence Platform

**Proposed for:** Ministry of Women & Child Development, Government of India  
**Purpose:** Strengthen evidence-based policy, service delivery, inter-departmental convergence and protection outcomes for women and children.

### **1\. Executive Summary**

India has an extensive ecosystem of laws, schemes, helplines, institutions and welfare programmes for women and children.

The challenge is no longer only **whether a government scheme exists**.

The larger challenge is:

> **Are women and children actually receiving the right intervention at the right time, and where is the system failing?**

The proposed **AI-Powered Women & Child Protection, Policy & Service Gap Intelligence Platform** will connect information from government reports, schemes, policies, service-delivery systems and other authorised datasets to identify:

* Emerging problems affecting women and children  
* Major offences and vulnerabilities  
* Geographic hotspots  
* Gaps in government service delivery  
* Under-utilisation of existing schemes  
* Capacity shortages  
* Inter-departmental coordination failures  
* Delays in intervention  
* Poor outcomes despite service availability  
* Emerging threats not adequately covered by existing policies  
* Policy and implementation gaps

The platform will then provide **evidence-backed recommendations to government officials**.

It will not replace police, courts, social workers, child protection professionals or government decision-makers.

It will provide the intelligence required for them to make better decisions.

# 6\. What Makes This Different?

This is **not another dashboard**.

A conventional dashboard answers:

> "How many cases occurred?"

The proposed platform answers:

> "Why are these cases occurring, why are existing interventions not producing the expected outcome, where is the system failing, and what should government consider doing?"

### **Example**

A conventional dashboard:

> Domestic violence cases: 82,000

AI Policy Intelligence:

> Domestic violence cases are increasing in selected districts.

> Existing services are available.

> However, service utilisation is low.

> The major gap appears to be accessibility and post-intervention support.

> Several districts have limited counselling capacity.

> Recommendation: strengthen district-level post-intervention support and establish outcome tracking.

This is the difference between **analytics** and **decision intelligence**.

# 10\. AI Gap Detection

The platform will classify detected gaps into categories.

### **Policy Gap**

Existing policy does not adequately address the problem.

### **Implementation Gap**

Policy exists but implementation is weak.

### **Access Gap**

Service exists but the beneficiary cannot access it.

### **Awareness Gap**

Eligible beneficiaries do not know about the service.

### **Capacity Gap**

Insufficient:

* Staff  
* Counsellors  
* Infrastructure  
* Shelter capacity  
* Funding  
* Technology

### **Geographic Gap**

Services are unavailable or difficult to reach.

### **Coordination Gap**

Multiple departments are responsible but coordination is weak.

### **Process Gap**

Cases are delayed between institutions.

### **Outcome Gap**

Service was delivered but the intended outcome was not achieved.

### **Data Gap**

Government does not have sufficient data to understand the problem.

### **Emerging Threat Gap**

A new threat exists that is not adequately addressed by current policy.

# **1\. Executive Summary**

India has laws, courts, police systems, helplines, protection mechanisms, welfare schemes, shelters, Child Welfare Committees, District Child Protection Units, One Stop Centres and multiple programmes for women and children.

Yet serious problems continue.

The fundamental challenge is therefore not simply:

> "Does a government scheme exist?"

The more difficult questions are:

> Why does the problem continue despite existing laws and schemes?

> Where does the protection system fail?

> Why does a woman or child fall through the system?

> Which intervention was supposed to happen but did not?

> Which department or institution is responsible for that missing intervention?

> Is the problem caused by policy, implementation, capacity, access, awareness, coordination, law, funding or social conditions?

> What should Government change?

The proposed system will create an **AI intelligence and intervention layer across the women and child protection ecosystem**.

It will continuously analyse authorised government data, policies, laws, schemes, reports, service-delivery information and evidence to identify:

**Problem → Vulnerability → Offence → Protection Journey → Intervention → Failure → Root Cause → Government Responsibility → Corrective Action → Outcome**

The objective is not to create another dashboard.

The objective is to create a system that helps Government **find problems that are otherwise difficult to see and determine what action should be considered.**

\*\*4. The Senior Software Engineer (Frontend/Backend Architect):\*\*   
"Building a multi-agent LLM swarm on live government databases is a security and reliability nightmare. We track anonymized 'Case State Tokens'. The frontend is not a dashboard; it is an 'Action Queue' optimized for low-bandwidth, offline-first mobile use by field workers (Anganwadi, Police, CWC members)."

\#\#\# THE FINAL SOLUTION: "PROJECT SURAKSHA SENTINEL"  
\*\*An Event-Driven Systemic Accountability & Intervention Engine\*\*

This is not a dashboard. It is an active, background AI application that monitors the \*health of the system\* by tracking anonymized case journeys and statutory deadlines, intervening before a failure becomes a tragedy.

\#\#\#\# CORE ARCHITECTURE (The "No-BS" Tech Stack)  
1\. \*\*Privacy-First Event Bus\*\*: When a case is registered (e.g., FIR at Police Station, Call to 1098/181), the system generates an \*\*Anonymized Case Token\*\* (e.g., \`CASE-DV-UP-LKO-8842\`). No names, no Aadhaar, no addresses enter the AI layer. Only metadata: \`Event Type\`, \`Timestamp\`, \`Current Department\`, \`Next Statutory Deadline\`, \`Required Action\`.  
2\. \*\*Deterministic State Machine\*\*: The AI does not "guess" what happens next. It follows a hardcoded, legally mandated workflow graph (e.g., FIR → Medical Exam → CWC Production → Legal Aid → Shelter).   
3\. \*\*The Sentinel Engine (Rule-Based \+ LLM)\*\*: Monitors the time elapsed between states. If \`Time \> Statutory Limit\`, it triggers an escalation.  
4\. \*\*Ground-Truth Verification (Citizen-in-the-Loop)\*\*: Automated, anonymous SMS/IVR in local languages to the registered mobile number: \*"Did the Protection Officer visit you within 3 days as per law? Reply 1 for Yes, 2 for No."\* This bypasses falsified departmental MIS data.

\#\#\# HOW IT SOLVES REAL PROBLEMS: A CONCRETE SCENARIO

\*\*The Real Problem\*\*: A 14-year-old girl is sexually abused. The police register a POCSO FIR. She is sent home because the local Child Care Institution (CCI) claims "no beds." She is intimidated by the accused's family and withdraws her statement. The system records this as "Case Closed: Lack of Evidence."

\*\*How SURAKSHA Sentinel Intervenes (Step-by-Step):\*\*

1\. \*\*T+0 Hours (Incident)\*\*: Police register FIR. System generates Token \`CASE-POCSO-BIH-9921\`. State: \`FIR\_REGISTERED\`. Next Deadline: \`CWC Production (24 hrs)\`.  
2\. \*\*T+12 Hours (AI Check)\*\*: System checks Health MIS API. No medical exam record linked to Token.   
   \- \*AI Action\*: Sends automated WhatsApp/SMS alert to the Station House Officer (SHO) and District Child Protection Officer (DCPO): \*"Statutory Warning: Medical exam for Token 9921 pending. 12 hours remaining before 24-hr JJ Act breach."\*  
3\. \*\*T+26 Hours (Breach Detected)\*\*: No CWC production recorded.   
   \- \*AI Action\*: Escalation Level 1\. Auto-drafts a formal intimation to the District Magistrate and the local Juvenile Justice Board (JJB) member, citing Section 31 of the JJ Act.   
4\. \*\*T+48 Hours (Systemic Block Identified)\*\*: DCPO replies via the system: \*"CCI Bed Unavailable."\*  
   \- \*AI Action\*: The Sentinel immediately queries the State WCD database for the nearest CCI with available capacity within a 50km radius. It auto-generates a transfer request and alerts the nearest functional CCI to prepare for admission.  
5\. \*\*T+7 Days (Ground Truth Check)\*\*: System triggers an anonymous IVR call to the guardian's registered number: \*"Has a counselor or protection officer spoken to the child since the incident?"\*   
   \- \*Response\*: "No."  
   \- \*AI Action\*: Flags a \*\*Service Delivery Gap\*\*. Escalates to the State Mission Director, highlighting that despite the CCI placement, psychosocial support (Mission Vatsalya mandate) has not been initiated.

\*\*The Result\*\*: The AI did not replace the police, the judge, or the social worker. It acted as an unyielding, tireless procedural watchdog that forced the system to follow its own laws, preventing the child from being sent home to an unsafe environment.

\# The Expert Panel's Verdict: Why the Previous Answer Fails

\#\# The core indictment

The previous answer proposed a "Gap-to-Decision Intelligence System" for senior officials. The panel's unanimous finding: \*\*senior officials are not the bottleneck.\*\* They have dashboards, reviews, coordination committees, and the power to summon any officer. What they lack is not intelligence about gaps. What they lack is \*\*a mechanism that forces a response at the point where a woman or child is falling out of the system\*\*—and that measures whether the response actually reached her.

The previous answer built a better map for people already holding the map. It did not build a vehicle.

\#\# The evidence the panel used to destroy it

\*\*1. The dashboard already exists, and it harms the intended beneficiary.\*\*

POSHAN Tracker was meant to be a monitoring tool. Instead, its facial-recognition system failed to recognise mothers, excluding eligible families from meals, preschool education, and health monitoring; government data cited only a \*\*52.7% ration delivery rate by end-2025\*\*. The CAG found that the abrupt transition from ICDS-CAS to Poshan Tracker disrupted data management, made outcome tracking impossible, and left Anganwadi workers without compatible smartphones. Anganwadi workers now enter over 150 data points daily, often with poor connectivity, and delays can cost them incentives.

\*\*The lesson for the panel:\*\* a system that measures the woman but does not serve her will, at scale, \*\*exclude her while appearing to govern her\*\*. The previous answer's "data architecture" had no answer for this.

\*\*2. Decision-support tools for officials are systematically ignored.\*\*

Behavioural research on Indian government AI adoption is unambiguous: "An official who follows an AI recommendation that departs from standard procedure" faces scrutiny; "an official who ignores the recommendation faces no such scrutiny". The officials who most need analytical support—ward officers, inspectors, junior engineers—are "often the least served by the systems built in their name".

\*\*The lesson for the panel:\*\* a tool whose primary user is the Secretary will be viewed, nodded at, and ignored. A tool that changes what a \*\*frontline worker can do tomorrow morning\*\* will be used.

\*\*3. The service journey is not broken because officials lack intelligence. It is broken because no actor is accountable for the transition.\*\*

The CPPR study of OSCs in three states found the absence of in-house Police Facilitating Officers, the absence of psychosocial counsellors in several districts, and \*\*a lack of robust MIS to track case progress or conduct vulnerability mapping\*\*. One Stop Centres are often perceived as "counselling or mediation centres" rather than medical and legal hubs; funding delays of six months to a year force staff to use personal funds.

\*\*The lesson for the panel:\*\* the gap is not a "coordination gap" to be visualised. It is a \*\*missing accountable handoff\*\* that no dashboard can create.

\*\*4. The law says what the panel already knew: legal aid is a right, not a referral.\*\*

The Chhattisgarh High Court held that merely advising a woman to approach the District Legal Services Authority, without ensuring actual and timely legal representation, violates Article 21\. Family courts cannot treat legal aid as a formality; they must maintain panels of lawyers who can be appointed immediately. Yet UNDP data shows \*\*87% of married women who experienced violence never sought help of any kind\*\*, and \*\*72% of women who faced a dispute and took no action said they did not know what to do\*\*.

\*\*The lesson for the panel:\*\* the problem is not that officials lack a "journey model." The problem is that the woman does not know her rights, does not have a lawyer when she needs one, and the system treats a referral as a solution. Any AI application must \*\*close the distance between the right and the remedy\*\*, not describe it.

\*\*5. The one AI application that works does something specific, for someone specific, with a clear response pathway.\*\*

Gujarat's AI-based Early Warning System predicted dropout risk with \*\*72% accuracy\*\* and helped prevent over \*\*1.67 lakh students\*\* from dropping out while re-enrolling over \*\*90,000\*\*; in Uttar Pradesh, attendance in pilot schools rose from \*\*48.8% to 62.8%\*\*. It works because it predicts a \*\*specific event\*\* (dropout), triggers a \*\*tiered response\*\* by a \*\*named actor\*\* (teacher/school), and connects the child to \*\*education, health, and social protection services\*\* where needed. It does not give officials a "gap score." It gives a teacher a list and a protocol.

\*\*The lesson for the panel:\*\* the previous answer's "Gap Score" and "Decision Centre" were abstractions. The Gujarat system is an \*\*intervention trigger\*\*.

\# The Rebuilt Product: What the Panel Would Actually Approve

\#\# The reframing

The previous answer positioned the product as "AI-Powered Women & Child Protection, Policy & Service Gap Intelligence." The panel rejects that category. It is a \*\*governance intelligence product\*\*—useful for the Ministry's own planning, but not a solution to the problem women and children face.

The rebuilt product is a \*\*Frontline Action and Accountability Engine\*\*. Its primary users are not the Minister or Secretary. Its primary users are:

1\. \*\*The woman or child herself\*\* (or a trusted intermediary acting for her: Anganwadi worker, ASHA, paralegal volunteer, NGO worker, teacher).  
2\. \*\*The frontline official who must act\*\* (OSC manager, CWC chairperson, Protection Officer, PFO, DLSA panel lawyer, school head).  
3\. \*\*The district-level convergence owner\*\* (Collector/DM) who is accountable for whether the handoff actually happened.

The senior official is a \*\*secondary user\*\*, who sees not "gaps" but \*\*unresolved cases and unanswered handoffs\*\*—a list of women and children for whom the system has failed to close the loop.

\#\# The product's three irreducible functions

\#\#\# Function 1: A rights-and-remedy navigator that reaches the woman where she is

\*\*What it does:\*\* A multilingual, voice-first, WhatsApp-based assistant that (a) helps a woman understand whether what is happening to her is recognised by law, (b) tells her the \*\*one next step\*\* that is actionable today, and (c) with her consent, \*\*creates a case record that triggers a human follow-up\*\* by a named official.

\*\*Why this is not another chatbot:\*\* existing legal chatbots (Jagrit, Nyaya Setu, Jann16) provide information. They do not create accountability. This system's output is not an answer—it is a \*\*timed handoff\*\*: "A Protection Officer must contact you within 48 hours. If she does not, this case escalates."

\*\*The legal basis the judges on the panel insisted on:\*\* the right to legal aid under Article 21 is not satisfied by information or referral; it requires \*\*timely representation\*\*. The assistant must therefore connect to a \*\*named panel lawyer\*\*, not a generic DLSA office, and the case record must show whether the lawyer actually appeared.

\*\*The evidence base:\*\* 72% of women who took no action did not know what to do; 87% of abused married women never sought help. The first barrier is \*\*invisibility of the remedy\*\*, not absence of a scheme.

\#\#\# Function 2: An unignorable case record that forces the handoff

\*\*What it does:\*\* every interaction that enters the system—whether through the woman's assistant, a helpline, an OSC, a CWC, a school, or a police station—creates a \*\*single case token\*\*. The token travels with her. At each handoff point (police → medical → legal → shelter → court → follow-up), the receiving actor must \*\*accept or reject the handoff with a timestamp and a reason\*\*. The case cannot be closed by the referring actor; it can only be closed by the \*\*receiving actor confirming that the service was delivered or the case was resolved\*\*.

\*\*Why this is different from an MIS:\*\* the CPPR study found that OSCs lack robust MIS to track case progress. But an MIS is a reporting tool; officials can leave fields blank. A \*\*handoff-acceptance protocol\*\* is an accountability tool: an unaccepted handoff is visible not as a "gap" but as a \*\*pending case with a named overdue officer\*\*. The district Collector's view is not a heatmap; it is a list: "These 47 women are waiting for a medical report; these 23 children are waiting for a CWC order; these 12 cases have no legal aid lawyer assigned after 7 days."

\*\*The evidence base:\*\* the CPPR study documented the absence of in-house PFOs and counsellors, funding delays, and the perception of OSCs as mediation centres. These are not knowledge gaps; they are \*\*handoff failures\*\*. A system that makes the handoff visible and attributable changes the incentive structure that behavioural research shows otherwise defaults to inaction.

\#\#\# Function 3: Outcome verification, not service counting

\*\*What it does:\*\* the system asks a different question at closure: not "Was a service delivered?" but "\*\*Did the woman or child reach the intended outcome?\*\*" For a domestic violence case: Is she safe at 3 months? Does she have legal representation? Has the protection order been complied with? For a child in CCI: Is she in school? Has family reunification been assessed? For a dropout-prevention case: Is the child still enrolled at 6 months?

\*\*Why this matters:\*\* the previous answer proposed an "Outcome Analysis Agent." The panel rejected this as an analytical exercise. Outcome verification must be a \*\*field function\*\*: a structured follow-up call or visit, recorded by the frontline worker, with the woman's or child's own assessment where possible. The data generated is not for a national dashboard; it is for the \*\*district convergence meeting\*\*, where unresolved outcomes are assigned to the officer responsible for the next action.

\*\*The evidence base:\*\* the CAG found that convergence between ICDS and NHM could not be assessed because there were no beneficiary-level service records. POSHAN Tracker measures attendance and growth monitoring, not whether the child's nutrition outcome improved. The system must measure \*\*what happened to the person\*\*, not what was recorded about her.

\#\# The legal architecture the judges required

The panel's legal members insisted that the application must be built \*\*inside the existing legal framework\*\*, not as a parallel techno-system.

1\. \*\*Consent and confidentiality.\*\* Every case record must be created with informed consent, in the woman's or child's language, with a clear explanation of who will see what. For children, the JJ Act confidentiality provisions apply; the system must enforce role-based access at the \*\*field level\*\*, not just at the dashboard level.

2\. \*\*No automated decision.\*\* The system may flag a handoff as overdue; it may not determine eligibility, credibility, custody, or guilt. The CWC, the court, the Protection Officer, and the lawyer decide.

3\. \*\*Legal aid as a right, not a referral.\*\* The system's legal module must connect to \*\*named panel lawyers\*\* and record whether representation was actually provided. The Chhattisgarh High Court's standard—that referral without timely representation violates Article 21—must be the system's design requirement.

4\. \*\*Evidence preservation for emerging threats.\*\* For cyberstalking, image-based abuse, deepfake CSAM, and sextortion, the system must include a \*\*one-touch evidence preservation\*\* function that creates a hash-stamped record and routes it to the cybercrime cell, without requiring the victim to repeatedly view or describe the material.

# **AI-Powered Women & Child Protection, Policy & Service Gap Intelligence**

Not as a generic dashboard.

\*\*Analytical framing.\*\* The WCD ecosystem already produces substantial data and operates multiple digital platforms. The unsolved problem is not data scarcity but \*\*asymmetric intelligence\*\*: the Ministry can measure \*what it delivers\* (Anganwadi beneficiaries, children in CCIs, OSC footfall) far better than it can measure \*whether delivery changes the trajectory of a woman’s or child’s life\*. The platform proposed below is designed to close that asymmetry—not by building another dashboard, but by building a \*\*gap-to-decision pipeline\*\* that connects an individual’s journey across WCD, police, health, courts, and community institutions, and forces every recommendation to carry its evidence, its responsible department, and its measurement plan.

\#\# 1\. Executive Summary

\*\*The core finding.\*\* India’s WCD architecture has strong \*scheme coverage\* and weak \*outcome observability\*. Mission Shakti, Mission Vatsalya, and Mission Saksham Anganwadi & POSHAN 2.0 collectively reach crores of beneficiaries, but the system cannot reliably answer a simple official question: \*for a woman or child who enters the system, where does the journey break, and which department owns that break?\*

\*\*\[EVIDENCE\]\*\* WCD’s Annual Report 2024-25 covers women’s empowerment, child development, child protection, CARA, NCW, and NCPCR across separate chapters, but there is no integrated, case-level journey dataset linking a report to a service to an outcome.  Mission Vatsalya’s own dashboard “reflects gaps in ensuring the minimum institutional framework across districts.”  A UNFPA assessment of One Stop Centres found persistent staffing and infrastructure gaps, privacy failures during counselling, and delays in medical reports that delay services.  A CAG performance audit found baseline surveys were not conducted in any audited district, making it impossible to know how eligible children were identified.  Mission Vatsalya awareness is “dismally low” across sampled states, with no state exceeding 50% awareness. 

\*\*\[INFERENCE\]\*\* The gap is structural: the Ministry monitors \*scheme inputs\* and \*institutional presence\*, not \*beneficiary trajectories\*. A girl may be counted in a CCI, in a school, and in a POSHAN Tracker record, but no system detects that she left the CCI to a family that then withdrew her from school and arranged a marriage.

\*\*\[RECOMMENDATION\]\*\* Build an \*\*AI-Powered Women & Child Protection, Policy & Service Gap Intelligence Platform\*\* whose primary output is not a chart but a \*\*prioritised, evidence-linked decision brief\*\* for the Minister, Secretary, and District Collector. The platform’s central screen asks \*“What should government do now?”\* and answers with top gaps, root causes, responsible departments, and KPIs.

\*\*\[DATA GAP\]\*\* No publicly available dataset currently links individual WCD service records to police FIRs, court case outcomes, school enrolment, or health records at the individual level. Privacy law and departmental silos make this integration legally complex. The platform must be designed to operate first at \*\*aggregate and cohort level\*\*, with individual-level linkage only where statutory authority and consent exist.

\#\# 5\. The Unsolved Government Problem

The unanswered question is not \*“How many schemes exist?”\* or \*“How many beneficiaries were reached?”\* The unanswered question is:

\> \*\*For a given problem in a given district, across the full journey from incident to outcome, where does the system lose the woman or child, which department owns that loss point, and what is the smallest operational or policy change that would move the outcome?\*\*

This is a \*\*gap-to-decision problem\*\*, not a reporting problem. It requires four capabilities that no existing WCD dashboard possesses:

1\. \*\*Journey reconstruction\*\* at cohort/aggregate level across departments.  
2\. \*\*Gap classification\*\* with explicit methodology (policy, implementation, access, awareness, capacity, geographic, coordination, process, outcome, data, emerging threat).  
3\. \*\*Root-cause tracing\*\* that does not stop at correlation.  
4\. \*\*Evidence-bound recommendation\*\* with responsible department, KPI, and monitoring method.

\*\*\[INFERENCE\]\*\* The scheme map reveals a consistent pattern: \*\*the earlier stages of the journey (awareness, reporting, intake) are relatively well-instrumented; the later stages (rehabilitation, reintegration, follow-up, outcome verification) are either absent from scheme design or not measured.\*\* OSC data counts women assisted; it does not count women who returned to the same violent household six months later. CCI data counts children in care; it does not count children who left care and re-entered the system.

\#\# 9\. Service Journey Model

\#\#\# 9.1 Woman’s journey (violence)

Incident → Awareness of entitlement → Reporting (181/OSC/police) → Emergency response → Medical examination → Psycho-social counselling → Legal aid → Shelter (if needed) → Court proceedings → Compensation → Rehabilitation/skilling → Reintegration → \*\*Follow-up (6/12/24 months) → Outcome verification\*\*

\*\*Fall-out points (evidence-based):\*\*

\- \*\*Awareness → Reporting:\*\* Mission Vatsalya awareness below 50% everywhere; POSH complaints extremely low.   
\- \*\*Reporting → Medical:\*\* medical report delays delay services.   
\- \*\*Counselling → Legal:\*\* privacy failures during counselling undermine trust.   
\- \*\*Court → Compensation:\*\* judicial backlog (12 lakh family court cases).   
\- \*\*Rehabilitation → Reintegration:\*\* no systematic follow-up data.

\#\#\# 9.2 Child’s journey (care and protection)

Incident/report → 1098/ERSS-112 → Rescue → CWC production → Medical → Police (if POCSO) → CCI/shelter → Education → Counselling → Family assessment → Reunification/foster/adoption/sponsorship → Aftercare → Reintegration → \*\*Follow-up → Outcome\*\*

\*\*Fall-out points:\*\*

\- \*\*Identification → CWC:\*\* baseline surveys absent; eligibility identification unclear.   
\- \*\*CWC → CCI:\*\* capacity and staffing shortages; overcrowding.   
\- \*\*CCI → Education:\*\* 45% of CCIs without counsellor; education quality unmeasured at outcome level.   
\- \*\*CCI → Family/adoption:\*\* 3.5-year adoption wait for infants.   
\- \*\*Aftercare → Reintegration:\*\* aftercare homes limited; no longitudinal outcome tracking.

\*\*\[RECOMMENDATION\]\*\* The platform’s journey model should treat \*\*follow-up and outcome verification as mandatory journey stages\*\*, not optional afterthoughts. Every scheme should be mapped to a \*minimum follow-up window\* and a \*minimum outcome indicator set\*.

\#\# 10\. Gap Taxonomy

The platform should detect eleven gap types, each with an explicit operational definition and a data requirement.

\| Gap type \| Definition \| Example from evidence \| Data requirement \|  
\|---\|---\|---\|---\|  
\| \*\*Policy gap\*\* \| No adequate policy or legal instrument exists for a documented problem \| AI-generated CSAM, deepfake exploitation: legislative framework “under consideration”  \| Legal gap analysis; emerging-threat scanning \|  
\| \*\*Implementation gap\*\* \| Policy exists, delivery is weak \| Baseline survey not conducted despite ICPS requirement  \| Scheme guideline vs. field audit \|  
\| \*\*Access gap\*\* \| Service exists but eligible person cannot reach it \| OSC not operational in all districts; some function 6–8 hours  \| Facility geolocation \+ timings \+ transport \|  
\| \*\*Awareness gap\*\* \| Eligible person does not know entitlement \| Mission Vatsalya awareness 1.1%–1.4% in large states  \| Survey data \+ helpline call-origin analysis \|  
\| \*\*Capacity gap\*\* \| Staff, infrastructure, funds, or technology insufficient \| 54% CCI staff shortage; 80% CCIs no medical staff  \| HR audits; CCI grading; fund utilisation \|  
\| \*\*Geographic gap\*\* \| Service unavailable or distant in specific locations \| Mission Vatsalya dashboard reflects gaps in minimum institutional framework across districts  \| District facility mapping; travel-time analysis \|  
\| \*\*Coordination gap\*\* \| Multiple departments responsible but handoffs fail \| OSC–police–health–legal coordination weaknesses  \| Journey timestamp data; handoff latency \|  
\| \*\*Process gap\*\* \| Case delayed between institutions \| Medical report delays; fund disbursement delays of 6–12 months  \| Process-mining on case timestamps \|  
\| \*\*Outcome gap\*\* \| Service delivered but intended outcome not achieved \| No follow-up data on violence recurrence or reintegration stability \| Longitudinal cohort tracking \|  
\| \*\*Data gap\*\* \| Government cannot measure the problem reliably \| No baseline for children in need; no real-time outreach tracking  \| Data availability audit \|  
\| \*\*Emerging threat gap\*\* \| New problem developing faster than policy \| Deepfake CSAM; algorithmic trafficking  \| Horizon scanning; cybercrime trend analysis \|

\#\# 11\. Root Cause Model

For every major issue, the platform should force a \*\*causal chain\*\*, not a dashboard description. The method should combine 5-Whys with process, geographic, demographic, and capacity analysis.

\*\*Example: child marriage reports rising in a district\*\*

\- \*\*Why?\*\* Higher reporting or higher incidence? (Data gap: distinguish reporting artefact from true increase.)  
\- \*\*Why are girls at risk?\*\* School dropout, economic vulnerability, lack of local secondary school, weak early detection.  
\- \*\*Why does early detection fail?\*\* Village/Block Child Protection Committees not functioning; Anganwadi workers not linked to school attendance data; CMPO vacancies.  
\- \*\*Why does the family choose marriage?\*\* Economic distress, dowry expectations, perceived insecurity of girls, lack of viable alternatives.  
\- \*\*Why do existing schemes not prevent it?\*\* BBBP awareness activity is not targeted at highest-risk cohorts; Nari Adalat and OSC are response-oriented, not prevention-oriented.  
\- \*\*Root cause candidates:\*\* \*absence of a functioning early-warning cohort\* (girls with dropout \+ economic vulnerability \+ sibling marriage history) and \*weak convergence between WCD, Education, and Panchayat\*.

\*\*\[EVIDENCE for causal factors\]\*\* NCPCR’s vulnerability mapping identified over 11.5 lakh at-risk children; Mission Vatsalya awareness is low; CAG found baseline identification absent. 

\*\*\[RECOMMENDATION\]\*\* The Root Cause Agent should output a \*\*causal graph with confidence levels\*\*, not a single “root cause.” Officials should see which links are evidenced, which are inferred, and which are data gaps.

\#\# 12\. AI Agent Architecture

The platform should be built as a \*\*multi-agent system with a human decision layer\*\*. No agent makes a decision affecting an individual’s liberty, custody, benefits, or guilt.

\| 1 \| \*\*Document Intelligence Agent\*\* \| Ingest WCD reports, scheme guidelines, SOPs, audit reports, Parliamentary answers, laws, judgments; extract structured metadata \| PDF parsing, OCR, layout-aware extraction, legal citation parser \| Document knowledge cards (§2 schema) \|  
\| 2 \| \*\*Issue Discovery Agent\*\* \| Detect problems not adequately represented in existing taxonomy; cluster emerging themes from audits, helpline data, court judgments \| Topic modelling, anomaly detection, expert-curated seed taxonomy \| New issue candidates with evidence \|  
\| 3 \| \*\*Offence Intelligence Agent\*\* \| Maintain offence cards; map offences to laws, authorities, services, expected journey, failure points \| Legal database, NCRB, scheme guidelines \| Structured offence intelligence \|  
\| 4 \| \*\*Scheme Matching Agent\*\* \| Match problem/beneficiary cohort to available schemes and services; flag “no scheme” cases \| Scheme knowledge graph, eligibility rules \| Scheme mapping \+ gap flag \|  
\| 5 \| \*\*Beneficiary Journey Agent\*\* \| Reconstruct aggregate journey funnels; identify drop-off points \| Programme data, helpline data, audit findings, judicial pendency \| Journey funnel with fall-out probabilities \|  
\| 6 \| \*\*Geographic Gap Agent\*\* \| District/block-level facility mapping; travel-time analysis; service-desert detection \| Facility geolocation, census population, road networks, POSHAN Tracker, Mission Vatsalya dashboard \| Geographic gap map \|  
\| 7 \| \*\*Service Capacity Agent\*\* \| Staffing, infrastructure, fund utilisation, caseload per worker \| CAG/audit data, CCI grading, HR records (where available) \| Capacity score by district/facility \|  
\| 8 \| \*\*Outcome Analysis Agent\*\* \| Assess whether delivered services are associated with improved outcomes at cohort level \| NFHS, programme data, follow-up data (where available), research literature \| Outcome association with caveats \|

\| 10 \| \*\*Policy Gap Agent\*\* \| Compare problem severity and trajectory against policy instruments and scheme design \| Legal database, scheme guidelines, Parliamentary committee reports \| Policy gap register \|  
\| 11 \| \*\*Legal Research Agent\*\* \| Map offences to statutes, amendments, judgments, and pending legislative proposals \| India Code, SCC Online (licensed), Supreme Court/HC judgments \| Legal anchor and interpretive risk \|  
\| 12 \| \*\*Emerging Threat Agent\*\* \| Monitor cybercrime trends, deepfake incidents, AI-generated CSAM, trafficking patterns \| NCRB cybercrime data, CERT-In advisories, MeitY consultations, open-source threat intelligence \| Emerging threat brief \|  
\| 13 \| \*\*Cross-Ministry Convergence Agent\*\* \| Map handoffs between WCD, Police, Health, Education, Judiciary, Legal Services, Panchayats, NGOs; identify broken links \| Departmental process maps, MoUs, integrated helpline data \| Convergence graph with broken edges \|

\#\#\# 13.2 RAG design principles

\*\*\[RECOMMENDATION\]\*\* The RAG system must be \*\*jurisdiction-aware, date-aware, and hierarchy-aware\*\*. A recommendation for a district in Rajasthan must retrieve Rajasthan state rules, not just central scheme guidelines. A legal statement must be anchored to the version of the Act in force at the relevant date. A scheme eligibility rule must reflect the latest amendment.

\*\*Mandatory evidence passport on every AI output:\*\*

\`\`\`  
EVIDENCE: \[quoted or paraphrased source statement\]  
SOURCE: \[document title, issuing authority, date, page/paragraph\]  
CONFIDENCE: \[HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW\]  
REASONING SUMMARY: \[why this source supports this claim\]  
DATA LIMITATIONS: \[what the source does not tell us\]  
\`\`\`

\*\*\[DATA GAP\]\*\* Tier 4 sources are the most valuable for journey intelligence and the least accessible. The platform must explicitly model \*\*access constraints\*\* and never imply that an unavailable dataset has been analysed.

\#\# 16\. Dashboard Architecture

The dashboard must \*\*not\*\* be a generic analytics dashboard. It should have six views, each with a specific decision purpose.

\#\#\# View 1: National Situation

\- \*\*Objective:\*\* Give the Minister/Secretary a single-screen evidence-based picture of the national trajectory, distinguishing reported trends from data-system artefacts.  
\- \*\*Users:\*\* Minister, Secretary, Additional Secretary.  
\- \*\*KPIs:\*\* NCRB crime rates (women, children) with trend arrows; programme coverage (POSHAN, OSC, CCI); staffing vacancy rates; adoption wait time; helpline call-to-assistance conversion.  
\- \*\*AI insights:\*\* “Crime against children rose 5.9% in 2024 while crime against women declined 1.5%; the divergence is concentrated in kidnapping/abduction and POCSO—not in a uniform deterioration.”   
\- \*\*Charts/maps:\*\* National trend lines with confidence bands; state heatmap of staffing vacancies; district facility-desert map.  
\- \*\*Alerts:\*\* Sudden divergence between reporting and service utilisation; capacity thresholds.  
\- \*\*Drill-down:\*\* State → district → facility.  
\- \*\*Recommended action:\*\* “Prioritise capacity audit in districts with \>50% CCI staff vacancy and rising child crime.”

\#\#\# View 2: Issue Explorer

\- \*\*Objective:\*\* Allow an official to select an issue (e.g., child marriage, domestic violence, deepfake abuse) and see the full gap picture: legal anchor, scheme map, journey fall-out, root causes, evidence.  
\- \*\*Users:\*\* Joint Secretary, Director, programme managers.  
\- \*\*KPIs:\*\* Issue-specific severity, coverage, gap score.  
\- \*\*AI insights:\*\* “Child marriage vulnerability is concentrated in districts with high school dropout and low Mission Vatsalya awareness; existing interventions are response-heavy and prevention-light.”  
\- \*\*Charts/maps:\*\* Issue-specific district heatmap; journey funnel.  
\- \*\*Alerts:\*\* Issue severity crossing threshold; new issue detected by Issue Discovery Agent.

\#\#\# View 3: Women/Child Journey

\- \*\*Objective:\*\* Show the funnel from incident/report to outcome, with drop-off points and responsible departments.  
\- \*\*Users:\*\* Programme managers, DCPU officers, state WCD.  
\- \*\*KPIs:\*\* Conversion rate at each stage; median time between stages; unresolved cases.  
\- \*\*AI insights:\*\* “Of 100 women who contact 181, fewer than 40 receive direct assistance; medical report delay is the largest process gap in OSC cases.”  
\- \*\*Charts:\*\* Stage-by-stage funnel; handoff latency box plots.  
\- \*\*Alerts:\*\* Stage conversion dropping below baseline; handoff latency exceeding threshold.

\#\#\# View 4: Government Scheme Gap

\- \*\*Objective:\*\* Show, for each scheme, the distance between designed coverage and operational coverage, and the distance between operational coverage and outcome.  
\- \*\*Users:\*\* Scheme managers, budget officers, evaluation units.  
\- \*\*KPIs:\*\* Budget utilisation; beneficiary reach vs. eligible population; staffing vs. sanctioned; outcome indicator movement.  
\- \*\*AI insights:\*\* “Mission Shakti budget increased 117% from RE 2024-25 to BE 2025-26, but OSC staffing gaps and low helpline conversion suggest absorption capacity is the binding constraint, not allocation.”   
\- \*\*Charts:\*\* Budget vs. utilisation vs. vacancy; scheme coverage vs. outcome.

\#\#\# View 5: District Gap Intelligence

\- \*\*Objective:\*\* Rank districts by composite gap severity and explain \*why\* each district is high-priority.  
\- \*\*Users:\*\* District Collectors, state secretaries, Ministry’s regional desks.  
\- \*\*KPIs:\*\* Composite gap score; sub-scores (capacity, access, coordination, outcome); trend.  
\- \*\*AI insights:\*\* “District X ranks high on capacity gap and coordination gap but low on access gap—the problem is not facility absence but staffing and handoff failure.”  
\- \*\*Maps:\*\* District heatmap with drill-down to facility.  
\- \*\*Alerts:\*\* District deterioration; persistent gap despite intervention.

\#\#\# View 6: Minister/Secretary Decision Centre

\- \*\*Objective:\*\* The “What should government do now?” screen. \*\*This is the most important screen.\*\* It presents the top 10 critical gaps, each with the full decision brief.  
\- \*\*Users:\*\* Minister, Secretary, senior advisors.  
\- \*\*Display for each gap:\*\*

\`\`\`  
PROBLEM → EVIDENCE → AFFECTED POPULATION → ROOT CAUSE  
→ EXISTING RESPONSE → MISSING INTERVENTION  
→ RESPONSIBLE DEPARTMENTS → RECOMMENDED ACTION  
→ EXPECTED OUTCOME → KPI → MONITORING METHOD  
→ CONFIDENCE → DATA LIMITATIONS  
\`\`\`

\- \*\*Interaction:\*\* One-click to “Issue Explorer” for deep dive; one-click to “District Gap Intelligence” for geographic detail; one-click to export decision brief.

\#\# 17\. Minister/Secretary Decision Centre

\*\*\[RECOMMENDATION\]\*\* The Decision Centre should be designed around \*\*decision latency\*\*, not data volume. A senior official should be able to move from \*“What is the most important gap?”\* to \*“Who owns it?”\* to \*“What will we measure?”\* within three clicks.

\*\*Design rules:\*\*

1\. \*\*No gap without an owner.\*\* Every gap must have a named responsible department or a explicit “coordination owner” (e.g., DCPU for cross-department child cases).  
2\. \*\*No recommendation without a measurement plan.\*\* Every action must specify KPI, data source, frequency, and baseline.  
3\. \*\*No claim without evidence grade.\*\* Each statement in the brief carries \`\[EVIDENCE\]\`, \`\[INFERENCE\]\`, \`\[RECOMMENDATION\]\`, or \`\[DATA GAP\]\`.  
4\. \*\*No alarmist language.\*\* Severity is expressed as a score and a trend, not as an emotive adjective.  
5\. \*\*No autonomous decision.\*\* The AI recommends; the official decides.

\#\# 18\. Policy Recommendation Engine

\#\#\# 18.1 Mandated output format

Every recommendation follows the 20-field format specified in the prompt: PROBLEM, EVIDENCE, AFFECTED POPULATION, CURRENT POLICY, CURRENT GOVERNMENT RESPONSE, IDENTIFIED GAP, ROOT CAUSE, RESPONSIBLE DEPARTMENT, POLICY OPTIONS, RECOMMENDED ACTION, IMPLEMENTATION PLAN, ESTIMATED COMPLEXITY, EXPECTED OUTCOME, KPI, MONITORING METHOD, RISKS, DATA LIMITATIONS, CONFIDENCE LEVEL.

\#\#\# 18.2 Evidence discipline

\*\*\[RECOMMENDATION\]\*\* The engine should enforce four rules:

\- \*\*Rule 1: Source-date-jurisdiction triple.\*\* No legal or scheme claim without all three.  
\- \*\*Rule 2: Negative evidence acknowledged.\*\* If a document is silent on an issue, the recommendation must say “no evidence found in retrieved sources” rather than implying absence of the problem.  
\- \*\*Rule 3: Confidence calibrated to evidence tier.\*\* Tier 1–2 sources support HIGH confidence; Tier 4–6 support MEDIUM or LOW; absence of data is \`\[DATA GAP\]\`, not LOW confidence.  
\- \*\*Rule 4: Counter-evidence surfaced.\*\* If a source contradicts the recommendation, it must be listed under RISKS.

\#\# 19\. Example Government Decision Scenarios

\#\#\# Scenario 1: Rising child marriage reports in District X

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* NCRB district child marriage cases rising; school dropout data from UDISE+ (if accessible); CWC case counts; NCPCR vulnerability mapping.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- \*\*Hotspot:\*\* Blocks with high female secondary-school dropout, high poverty, low Anganwadi-to-school transition.  
\- \*\*Age group:\*\* 14–17.  
\- \*\*Correlation:\*\* Dropout precedes marriage reports by 6–12 months.  
\- \*\*Existing interventions:\*\* BBBP awareness, CWC, CMPO, police.  
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* No early-warning cohort; awareness activity not targeted; CMPO vacancies; weak school–WCD data sharing.  
\- \*\*Root cause:\*\* Absence of a functioning \*\*girl-at-risk register\*\* linking dropout, economic vulnerability, and sibling marriage history; weak convergence between Education and WCD.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Establish a district-level \*\*Girl-at-Risk Cohort\*\* using existing UDISE+ and Anganwadi data; assign tracked follow-up to Anganwadi worker \+ school teacher \+ CMPO; measure secondary-school continuation at 12 months.  
\- \*\*Responsible:\*\* District Collector (convergence), WCD (CMPO), Education (dropout data), Panchayat (community monitoring).  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Percentage of identified at-risk girls still in school at 12 months; child marriage reports per 10,000 adolescent girls.

\#\#\# Scenario 2: High OSC footfall but low case resolution

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* OSC monthly case counts; conversion to legal aid; medical report turnaround time; court pendency.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- OSC footfall is high, but \<40% of helpline calls result in direct assistance.   
\- Medical report delays are the largest process gap.   
\- Police facilitating officers absent in some OSCs.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Process gap \+ coordination gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Integrate OSC case management with district hospital medical report workflow; mandate PFO presence; measure median time from OSC intake to medical report and to legal aid.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Median days from intake to medical report; percentage of cases with legal aid within 7 days.

\#\#\# Scenario 3: CCI overcrowding and staff shortage in District Y

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* CCI grading data; sanctioned vs. actual staff; children against capacity.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- Excess children 14–438% against capacity in audited CCIs.   
\- 80% of responding CCIs had no medical staff.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Capacity gap \+ outcome gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Immediate medical staff deployment via district hospital MoU; cap intake; fast-track family reunification/sponsorship for children with families; measure CCI occupancy and medical staff presence monthly.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* CCI occupancy ratio; percentage of CCIs with medical staff; reunification rate.

\#\#\# Scenario 4: Deepfake CSAM emerging in District Z

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* Cybercrime complaints; MeitY/MHA advisories; Issue Discovery Agent flag.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- Deepfake/AI-generated CSAM challenges existing legal categories.   
\- Legislative framework under consideration, not yet operational.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Policy gap \+ emerging threat gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Interim protocol for cybercrime cell \+ CWC \+ CCI for AI-CSAM cases; evidence preservation SOP; victim support without requiring victim to view material; track case counts.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Number of AI-CSAM cases identified; time to evidence preservation; victim support uptake.

\#\#\# Scenario 5: Low PMMVY uptake among eligible tribal women

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* PMMVY beneficiary data vs. estimated births; tribal district gap.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- PMMVY has reached 4.27 crore beneficiaries, but uptake may lag in remote blocks.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Access gap \+ awareness gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Anganwadi-based assisted registration; mobile enrolment camps; measure uptake among tribal women.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* PMMVY uptake rate among eligible tribal women.

\#\#\# Scenario 6: High SHe-Box complaint backlog in a state

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* SHe-Box complaint counts; disposal rates; LC/IC constitution data.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- Only 85 of 265 complaints disposed nationally.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Process gap \+ capacity gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* District-wise LC performance dashboard; mandatory inquiry timeline; escalate non-disposal to District Officer.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Percentage of complaints disposed within 90 days.

\#\#\# Scenario 7: Adoption wait time for special-needs children

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* CARA data; waiting children; waiting PAPs.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- 3.5-year wait for infants; special-needs children wait longer.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Process gap \+ access gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Targeted subsidy/support for special-needs adoption; fast-track home study; measure time-to-placement by child category.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Median wait time by child category.

\#\#\# Scenario 8: Missing children not traced within 30 days

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* TrackChild/Mission Vatsalya portal; police missing-person data.

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- 98,375 children missing in 2024, up 7.8%.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Coordination gap \+ process gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Automatic escalation after 30 days; integration of 1098, 112, and police missing-person database; district-level tracing task force.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Percentage of missing children traced within 30/90 days.

\#\#\# Scenario 9: Low Nari Adalat case resolution

\*\*Data triggers:\*\* Nari Adalat pilot data (Assam, J\&K).

\*\*AI analysis:\*\*  
\- Nari Adalat is a pilot, not a formal court; referral pathways unclear.   
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Policy gap (scale-up design) \+ process gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Define referral criteria, legal aid linkage, and outcome measurement before national scale-up.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Case resolution rate; referral-to-legal-aid rate.

\#\#\# Scenario 10: District with high POSHAN coverage but high child malnutrition

\- Coverage is high, but outcome is poor.  
\- \*\*Gap:\*\* Outcome gap.  
\- \*\*Recommendation:\*\* Shift from coverage monitoring to \*\*outcome-linked cohort tracking\*\*; measure growth velocity, not just growth monitoring visits; investigate food quality and counselling quality.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Percentage of children moving from “at risk” to “normal” growth category over 6 months.

\#\# 29\. Demonstration Scenario

\#\#\# “Why are women in District X experiencing poor outcomes despite existing government services?”

\*\*Step 1: Data triggers\*\*

\- NCRB district data: domestic violence cases above state average.  
\- OSC data: high footfall, low legal-aid conversion.  
\- NFHS district factsheet: high anaemia, low female workforce participation.  
\- CAG/state audit: DCPU vacancy high; OSC counselling room shared.

\*\*Step 2: AI detects problem\*\*

\- \*\*Issue:\*\* Domestic violence response system is intake-heavy but resolution-light.  
\- \*\*Gap classification:\*\* Process gap (medical/legal delay) \+ capacity gap (counselling privacy, staffing) \+ coordination gap (OSC–police handoff).

\*\*Step 3: AI identifies existing schemes\*\*

\- Mission Shakti (OSC, WHL 181, Nari Adalat), PWDVA protection officer, legal aid, Shakti Sadan.

\*\*Step 4: AI maps service journey\*\*

\- Reporting → OSC intake → medical → counselling → legal aid → court → shelter → follow-up.  
\- \*\*Drop-off detected:\*\* 60% of OSC clients do not reach legal aid; medical report delay is the largest time gap.

\*\*Step 5: AI identifies missing intervention\*\*

\- No \*\*medical report fast-track\*\* for OSC cases; no \*\*mandatory PFO presence\*\*; no \*\*follow-up after court disposal\*\*.

\*\*Step 6: AI identifies responsible departments\*\*

\- WCD (OSC), Health (medical report), Police (PFO), Legal Services (legal aid), Judiciary (case tracking).

\*\*Step 7: Root cause analysis\*\*

\- \*\*Immediate:\*\* Medical report delay and PFO absence.  
\- \*\*Underlying:\*\* No service-level agreement between OSC and district hospital; no performance metric for PFO deployment.  
\- \*\*Structural:\*\* OSC success measured by footfall, not by legal resolution or safety outcome.

\*\*Step 8: Recommendation\*\*

\- \*\*Action:\*\* District Collector convenes OSC–hospital–police–DLSA meeting; adopts a 7-day medical report SLA; mandates PFO presence; introduces 6-month safety follow-up for OSC cases.  
\- \*\*KPI:\*\* Median days to medical report; percentage of OSC cases reaching legal aid within 7 days; percentage of cases with safety follow-up at 6 months.  
\- \*\*Responsible:\*\* District Collector (convergence), CMO (medical), SP (PFO), DLSA (legal aid), DPO/WCD (OSC).  
\- \*\*Complexity:\*\* Medium.  
\- \*\*Evidence:\*\* UNFPA OSC assessment; CAG staffing audit; NCRB district data.  
\- \*\*Data limitation:\*\* No individual-level outcome data; recommendation is based on aggregate and documented process failures.

\*\*Step 9: Minister/Secretary decision brief\*\*

\- One-page brief with problem, evidence, affected population, root cause, existing response, missing intervention, responsible departments, recommended action, expected outcome, KPI, monitoring method, confidence, and data limitations.  
\- \*\*Decision:\*\* Approve district pilot; direct WCD to issue OSC–hospital coordination guideline; add OSC outcome indicators to national dashboard.

\#\# 30\. Final Product Vision

The product is not a dashboard. It is a \*\*gap-to-decision intelligence system\*\* that helps the Government answer seven questions in sequence:

1\. \*\*What problem are women and children facing?\*\* → Issue taxonomy \+ evidence.

3\. \*\*Who is affected?\*\* → Cohort identification (aggregate).
